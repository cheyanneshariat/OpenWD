# D6 module

[Model guide](README.md) · [Getting started](../getting-started.md)

`compute_d6` solves a warm, hydrogen- and helium-free LTE atmosphere made of
carbon, oxygen and heavier elements, as found in the D6 supernova survivors.
Unlike DZ, the metals are not trace species in a helium pressure reservoir:
every element contributes nuclei, all loaded ionization stages, electrons and
mass to the same pressure, charge and mass closure. The defaults are the
Hollands et al. (2025, MNRAS 541, 2231) solution for SDSS J1637+3631:
Teff = 15680 K, log g = 6.3 and log number ratios to carbon O +0.27, Ne −1.86,
Mg −2.33, Al −4.00, Si −1.76, S −2.84, Ca −2.85, Fe −3.00.

```python
from wd_spectra import D6Config, run_model

run = run_model(D6Config(quality="standard"), "results/j1637")
print(run.convergence_verified)
```

`D6Config(abundances=..., reference_element="C")` takes log10 number ratios
to the reference element, whose own entry must be zero. Hydrogen and helium
entries are rejected; use DZ or DQ for helium-dominated mixtures.

## Physics

- **EOS.** Ideal multi-element Saha balance with charge neutrality and the
  particle equation solved together at fixed pressure. Partition functions
  stop 0.1 eV below each continuum (Koester 2010). Complete Stout ion ladders
  are kept for C, O and Ne because the deepest layers ionize them beyond
  charge +2; other elements carry three stages. ML2 convection (alpha 1.25)
  uses fixed-pressure derivatives of the same EOS, including ionization and
  excitation energy.
- **Continuum.** Level-resolved Opacity Project photoionization: TLUSTY
  28+12-level C I and 23+10-level O I atoms, SIROCCO/TOPbase C II and O II,
  and TLUSTY Ne I, Mg I–II, Al II, Si I–II, S II and Fe II. Experimental
  thresholds are applied to the TOPbase cross-section shapes. Every other
  loaded stage uses a Verner ground-state fit. Ionic free-free absorption and
  electron scattering complete the continuum. The expanded O I atom matters:
  the older seven-level atom has no optical threshold at all.
- **Lines.** Stout line lists for charges 0–2, plus the O I 6258–6271 Å
  multiplet from Kurucz GF100, which Stout omits. Broadening uses thermal,
  radiative, measured Stark widths where available (Ca II H/K, Mg II 4481,
  Mg I 3835, O I 4369/7775/8449) with a classical estimate otherwise, and
  Unsöld van der Waals. The O I 3p ⁵P–nd ⁵D series uses the
  Dimitrijević, Iacob & Sahal-Bréchot (2025) 4d width scaled as n_eff⁵ instead
  of the generic fallback, which caps n_eff at 5. High O I and Mg Rydberg lines
  are reduced by their Q-MHD survival probability, and the dissolved fraction
  is returned as a level-resolved pseudo-continuum below the TOPbase edge.
- **Linear Stark broadening of Rydberg lines.** At nₑ ≈ 10¹⁵–10¹⁶ cm⁻³ the ion
  microfield mixes nearly hydrogenic Rydberg levels (outer electron with
  l ≥ 2, converging on the ground parent) with their l ± 1 neighbours, and their
  lines acquire quasi-static linear-Stark profiles that impact widths omit.
  Each such line's impact profile is blended with the Holtsmark envelope of the
  hydrogenic linear-Stark splitting, (3/2)n(n−1)ea₀F₀/Z, weighted depth by depth
  by the two-level mixing fraction 1 − D/√(D² + S²). Here S is that splitting
  and D = 2RyZ²|Δδ|/n³ is the quantum-defect gap to the nearest existing l ± 1
  level. The oscillator strength is conserved and there is no adjustable
  parameter. This applies in both the structure and the spectrum; it moves the
  O I 3p–nd (n ≥ 8) series and the Mg II 4d–nf lines onto the observation.
- **Structure.** The bulk-metal EOS, ML2 thermodynamics and D6 opacity are
  handed to the shared adaptive trust-region Newton solver used by DA, DB, DAB
  and DZ, including its all-depth flux and local-energy certification. The
  default 25000 structural lines are ranked by abundance, line-forming ion
  fraction, lower-level population, oscillator strength and the Planck flux
  at the line, so the budget goes mostly to the ultraviolet where most of the
  flux emerges. The structure wavelength grid resolves each selected line's
  full profile support.
- **Line support.** Each line's profile extends until its omitted wing is
  optically thin (vertical optical depth 10⁻³ at the support edge) rather than
  stopping at 100 Lorentz half-widths. In a metal-dominated atmosphere the
  strongest resonance lines exceed the continuum by 10⁵ at line centre, so a
  fixed cutoff deletes wings comparable to the continuum; for J1637 it removed
  the Ca II H & K wings and 4–6 per cent of the Rosseland-mean opacity. The
  result is converged in this tolerance (10⁻⁴ changes the flux by < 0.3 per
  cent).
- **Synthesis.** The emergent spectrum uses the same piecewise-linear formal
  solution and exact coherent-scattering source as DZ, on a 3400–7700 Å vacuum
  grid by default.

Hollands et al. found C⁻/O⁻ opacity and CO, CO⁺ and O₂⁺ negligible at this
temperature, so they are not included. They will be needed for the cooler
(about 8000 K) D6 stars.

## Validation against J1637

The reference is a vector digitization of Hollands et al. Figure 1 (GTC
spectrum and their Koester-code model), bundled as
`wd_spectra/data/validation/hollands2025_j1637_figure1.npz`.
`wd_spectra.validation.hollands_d6.hollands2025_scores` convolves a model to
R = 2500, normalizes the four panels with a robust log-linear continuum and
reports the RMS difference from the digitized model and observation, plus the
paper's fifth-order re-fluxed comparison in the broad panel.

A second, *matched* panel metric divides out a smooth quadratic continuum
ratio between the model and each digitized panel before taking the RMS, like
the paper's own re-fluxing. Use it for physics comparisons. The historical
log-linear metric is kept for continuity, but it measures continuum placement
as much as lines: the digitized Koester model scores 0.030, 0.043, 0.004 and
0.010 against itself in panels 1–4. The digitized *observation* sits about
0.5–0.9 Å blueward of vacuum line centres (an imperfect rest-frame shift in
the figure), so the Koester panels are the cleaner regression target.

Public `run_model(D6Config())` cold start at standard quality (48 layers,
25000 structure and formal lines, September 2026, one thread):

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Certificate | all five structure-grid gates pass |
| All-depth flux / local energy | 5e-9 / 1.3e-4 (limits 3e-3) |
| Nonlinear iterations / wall time | 27 / 133 minutes |
| Matched RMS vs Koester model, panels 1–4 | 0.037, 0.033, 0.028, 0.029 (mean 0.0315) |
| Matched RMS vs GTC observation, mean | 0.056 |
| Historical log-linear RMS vs Koester / observation | 0.043 / 0.064 |

The first release, without the linear-Stark Rydberg envelopes and with the
100-half-width line cutoff, scored 0.033 and 0.058 (matched) and 0.044 and
0.065 (historical).

For comparison, the last development-tree reference, relaxed with an earlier
D6-specific solver, scores 0.036 (matched) and 0.041 (historical) against the
Koester model. That structure used a fixed column-mass grid reaching
tau_R ≈ 1e8 whose six deepest layers were never relaxed, and it certified flux
only above tau_R ≈ 10.

## Known differences and limitations

- LTE only. The ≈8000 K D6 stars need C⁻/O⁻ opacity and molecules; the hot
  D6 stars need NLTE.
- Stout oscillator strengths and approximate widths are not the Kurucz/VALD/
  NIST mixture of the Koester models. Hollands et al. report missing opacity
  near C I 4470, O I 6107 and Mg II 5403, and O I 5331/4970 widths that are
  too broad/narrow in their own model, so agreement with their model is not
  agreement with the observation in those features.
- The Holtsmark field distribution used for the linear-Stark envelope ignores
  radiator–perturber correlation, which reduces low fields around ionic
  radiators (Mg II, Al III, Si III, C III); those envelopes may be slightly too
  broad. O I 3p–nd 6d/7d remain deeper than observed, and 4d/5d about 0.04
  shallower; no current treatment explains both.
- O I lines with autoionizing upper levels (the ²D°/²P° excited-parent series
  above the O I limit: 6108, 6263–6271, 7159 and 7479–7483 Å) are too weak here
  and partly in the Koester model. Only radiative damping is included; an
  autoionization width of order 10¹¹–10¹² s⁻¹ would reproduce them, but
  adopted widths must come from atomic data (published autoionization widths
  or Opacity Project resonance widths), not from this fit.
- Ca II H & K now slightly exceed the observed depth 2 Å from line centre,
  while matching the far wings.
- Rydberg line dissolution is applied only to O and Mg, the elements whose
  dissolved strength is returned as a level-resolved pseudo-continuum. For
  the other elements, dissolving lines without returning the strength would
  lose opacity, and the continuation for them is not validated. This choice
  slightly deepens the blue high-series lines (matched RMS 0.0328 → 0.0333 at
  fixed structure). Where a TOPbase section opens above its nominal threshold
  the pseudo-continuum is still zero, so part of the dissolved O I strength
  near 5300–7000 Å is not returned.
- TOPbase C II/O II tables that match no parent channel of the next ion within
  0.4 eV (4 C II and 49 O II sections) are omitted; the spectrum metadata
  records this. Their optical contribution at J1637 is below 0.1 per cent.
- Level-resolved atoms are unavailable for Al I, S I, Ca I–II and Fe I; they
  keep Verner ground-state fits.
- The digitized reference is a plotted, re-fluxed model, not a machine-readable
  grid, and the solution is one tested point, not a Teff/log g/abundance grid.
